# GTM — Go-To-Market

---

## The Exact Target User

**Title:** CTO or Head of Engineering at a seed-to-Series-A startup, 8–35 people, technical founder still in the code.

**Why this specific person:** They are the one who set up the Cursor Business seats, added GitHub Copilot to the org, approved the OpenAI API key, and forwarded the Anthropic invoice to finance without reading it. They also get a Slack message from their co-founder or CFO every quarter: *"our AWS + AI bill is $4k this month, can you look at this?"* They don't have a procurement team. They don't have a FinOps function. They have a spreadsheet and a vague feeling that something is redundant.

They are not an enterprise buyer. They are a builder who accidentally became a budget owner.

## First 100 Users in 30 Days, $0 Budget

**Week 1: Seed with intent traffic, not reach**

1. **Run the tool on your own stack and publish the audit link publicly.** Write a 400-word post on X or HN with the real numbers: *"I was spending $X/month across 5 AI tools. Here's what the audit found."* Attach the shareable audit link. Don't sell — show the output.

2. **Search X daily for `"openai bill"`, `"cursor pro"`, `"copilot vs cursor"`, `"ai tools expensive"`.** Find people mid-frustration and reply: *"I built something for this exact problem — here's a link to your free audit."* Target: 10 direct replies per day.

3. **Post in r/ExperiencedDevs and r/SaaS** with a Show HN-style post. Title: *"I built a free tool that audits your AI tool stack and finds cheaper alternatives — here's what it found for mine."* Include the actual audit output. Don't bury the lede.

**Week 2: Convert communities with existing frustration**

4. **Drop into Cursor's Discord** in #general or #billing channels. When anyone asks *"is Pro worth it for a team of X?"* — answer helpfully, then add: *"I built a calculator that accounts for seat minimums and use-case fit if that's useful."*

5. **Email 20 YC S23/W24 CTOs directly.** The YC portfolio list is public. Filter for dev-tool or B2B SaaS companies at seed stage. Write a 4-sentence cold email: what it does, what it found for a comparable stack, the link, no ask.

6. **Post a Show HN** on a weekday morning. HN Show HN front-page = 200–800 visitors in 24 hours. Title: *"Show HN: I built a free AI tool spend auditor — enter your stack, get cheaper alternatives."*

**Week 3–4: Earned distribution**

7. **Find the 2–3 users who shared their audit link on their own.** DM them. Ask if they'd write one sentence about it. These are your first testimonials and referral loop seeds.

8. **Reach out to The Pragmatic Engineer, Lenny's Newsletter, TLDR Tech** with a data-led blurb: *"Across the first N audits, 70% of teams are paying for at least two overlapping coding assistants."*

## The Unfair Distribution Channel

**The shareable public audit link is the distribution.**

Every audit produces a public URL — a branded, read-only page with OG meta tags and savings summary. When a CTO shares their audit with their co-founder or posts it on Twitter to say *"look what I found"*, Credex is embedded in the share. The link *is* the referral.

No comparable tool generates a shareable artifact by default. Enterprise tools (CloudZero, Apptio, Zylo) are gated behind demo calls and never produce anything shareable. This tool produces a public link in under 2 minutes.

The higher the savings number, the more embarrassing and therefore interesting it is to share. The product is its own distribution engine.

## What Week-1 Traction Looks Like If This Works

- **200–400 unique visitors** in the first 7 days
- **60–90 audits submitted** (15–25% visitor-to-audit conversion; no login required)
- **12–20 email captures** from users who opted in
- **4–8 public audit links shared** organically
- **1–2 inbound lead captures** (users who clicked the high-savings CTA)
- **1 HN comment thread** with 10+ replies where someone says *"this is surprisingly accurate for my stack"*

If none of that happens in week 1, the problem is not the channel — it's the landing page copy or the audit output isn't credible enough. Both are fixable. A zero-engagement week is signal, not failure.
